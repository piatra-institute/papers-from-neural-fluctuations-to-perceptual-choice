# Research log

## Origin

A conversation that began with a slide on the free-energy principle and asked what its physical implementation would be, then whether sensation science lacks a state variable in the way thermodynamics lacked temperature. It proposed an "inferential temperature" defined as the ratio of spontaneous fluctuations of a neural decision variable to its response to a controlled evidence perturbation, first in an equilibrium fluctuation-dissipation form, Theta(tau) = -dC/dtau / R(tau), and then, after a review, in a first-passage form for bounded evidence accumulation, Theta = D / (M B), with the prediction that the psychometric slope equals M B / D. It named the public macaque Neuropixels dataset of Steinemann et al. (random-dot motion, LIP populations, randomized motion pulses) as the first test bed, proposed development and confirmation splits by monkey, a list of failure modes (observation noise in the decoded variable, first-passage selection bias, state-dependent diffusion and mobility, collapsing bounds and urgency, non-Markov dynamics), and a series of falsification gates. It ended by removing "temperature" and "Einstein" from the title.

## Questions

1. What exactly does the first-passage relation say, and under which transformations of the decoded variable, time and evidence is the comparison between a neural estimate and behaviour meaningful?
2. How do the standard departures from the pure integrator (leak or instability, trial-to-trial drift variability, start-point bias and variability, collapsing bounds or urgency) change the slope, and by how much?
3. Does the equilibrium fluctuation-response ratio along a decoded axis test anything? When is it constant in lag?
4. Can D, M and B be estimated from spiking populations without choices, despite absorbing bounds and observation noise? Which naive estimators fail, and by how much?
5. In simulated data with a known generating model, how close is the neural prediction to the slope fitted to choices, under the integrator and under departures, and which diagnostics computed without choices flag the departures?
6. How many trials does an equivalence test between prediction and behaviour need?

## Decisions

- Kept: the first-passage form of the proposal, the coordinate invariance of D/(MB), the use of randomized pulses, the separation of observation and process noise, the warning against choice-conditioned trajectories, the development/confirmation split by monkey, the hierarchy from response geometry to a scalar.
- Changed: the equilibrium fluctuation-dissipation construction is not used as a test. Along the Fisher discriminant of the stationary response of a linear system the fluctuation-response ratio is constant at every lag whether or not the system is at equilibrium, and along other readouts it varies even at equilibrium, so a "scalar collapse" along a trained decoder tests nothing. The estimators of D and M are rebuilt on optional stopping, which removes first-passage selection bias without a state-space model. The mobility is measured from sustained low-evidence trials as well as from pulses, because pulses of the simulated size give an imprecise estimate.
- Changed: the meaning of a successful comparison. In simulation the stopped estimators measure effective diffusion and mobility over the decision window and predict the slope under leak, instability, drift variability and urgency nearly as well as under the integrator; a pass therefore supports bounded accumulation with the measured effective ratio, not the pure integrator.
- Dropped: "temperature" and "Einstein relation" as claims; both appear only as the physical analogy the relation makes exact for the integrator. Dropped the free-energy framing; precision appears once as a quantity that could be compared with the measured ratio.
- No data were analyzed. The public dataset is described from its paper, and the analysis plan is stated for it.
